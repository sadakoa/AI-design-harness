github.com/sadakoa/AI-design-harness

A small starter kit for teams where people and AI agents design UI together. It is a repo of plain files plus three skills and a check script.

WHY

Why it exists

Design rules usually live in people's heads. The harness writes them down as small files, with three skills and one check, so an AI agent can follow them.

Today

❌ One long doc mixes rules, values and one-off decisions

❌ Review comments are opinions with nothing to point to

❌ What a review teaches is lost in old threads

With the harness

✅ One question, one file

✅ Every review finding points to a named rule

✅ Findings flow back and become rules

The usual setup is one long design document. It mixes everything and drifts. Review comments are opinions with no rule behind them, and what a review teaches rarely makes it back. The harness is a template repo that fixes those three things: small files with one job each, reviews tied to named rules, and a log that turns findings into rules.

AI Design Harness

3

Build turns a PRD into a one-page proposal. Review checks it one lens at a time. Anything that might matter beyond that screen goes into the feedback log. Once a week an owner runs promote-feedback, which sorts the log, and the owner decides what becomes a rule. Owners are the people listed in CODEOWNERS. The design system sits in the middle, and the next build reads from it.

Building and reviewing are different jobs, so they read different things. Building reads widely and spreads out into a few real options. Reviewing checks one area at a time — components, layout, then copy, meaning the UI text — and only re-reads the files for that area. Every finding points to a rule or principle ID.

AI Design Harness

7

Every finding starts open in work/feedback.md. Once a week promote-feedback groups them and suggests where each should go, but an owner decides. Adopted changes go in a PR with a row in decisions.md. Screen-only ones stay with that screen. Dropped ones keep their row and the reason.

design-builder walks through what a designer does in their head. The important part is step 5: options have to differ on real axes, not colors. For a brand-new kind of screen it stops at the options and a person picks. You'll answer up to five questions along the way.

IN PRACTICE

Running it as a team

Owners are the people listed in CODEOWNERS. They approve every change to the design system.

First months

Only owners change the design system. Decide in batches.

Then

Designers open PRs too. Start with copy and guidelines.

Every week

An owner runs promote-feedback and decides.

Approvals

With two or more owners, require their approval on GitHub.

NOT FOR

Brand-new kinds of screens. The agent drafts options; a designer should finish them.

AI Design Harness

9

Start small: owners only, decide in batches. Open it up once the output is steady. GitHub won't let you approve your own PR, so with one person, let a PR sit a day and reread the diff. Agents can draft options for brand-new screens, but they won't reach a shippable bar on their own.

APPENDIX

What the check catches

npm run check runs on your machine and on every pull request.

File missing from INDEX

A file not listed in INDEX.md, or a different question

tokens.css out of date

tokens.json changed but tokens.css wasn't rebuilt

Hard-coded colors

Raw colors in screens and options (R-01)

Unknown variables

CSS variables that aren't in tokens.css

Duplicate IDs

Two entries share an ID: P- principle, R- rule, D- decision, FB- feedback

Adopted without a decision

Feedback marked adopted with no row in decisions.md
