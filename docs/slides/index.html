AI Design Harness

Find out what's known, check you're building the right thing, then design it the way your team would — with an AI agent.

github.com/sadakoa/AI-design-harness

A plugin for teams where people and AI agents design product together. It adds a few folders of short notes to your repo: what you know about the product, your design rules, and a log of what you learned. Six skills use them and a check keeps them tidy, but nobody has to learn either: you just ask in your own words.

WHY

Why it exists

What we know about users and our design rules usually live in people's heads. The harness writes both down as short notes in your repo, so an AI agent can follow them. You just ask, in your own words.

The most expensive mistake is a clean design for the wrong problem, and nobody checks a PRD against what the team already knows. On top of that, the usual setup is one long design document. It mixes everything and drifts. Review comments are opinions with no rule behind them, and what a review teaches rarely makes it back. And every request starts from scratch, though the answer is often already in a doc or a chat somewhere. The harness fixes those five things: a look at what's known, a check before design, small files with one job each, reviews tied to named rules, and a log that turns lessons into changes.

WHAT YOU GET

One proposal per screen or flow

A plain answer first, then about three clickable mockups, a recommendation and a spec on one HTML page, reviewed against your rules. Your team still decides, and builds it.

AI Design Harness

4

A request comes in as a PRD, a line in Slack or a problem. Discover looks in your team's tools for what's already known. Frame checks it against product/: who it's for, which job, which past decisions. A go moves on to Build, which reads design-system/ and makes options and a spec. Review checks the pick against your rules, then your team decides and ships. Review findings, and what real use shows, go into the log. Owners sort it as features merge: product lessons into product/, design lessons into design-system/. The next request starts from there. Nobody types the commands; they're shown so you can find each step later.

IDEA 1

Look before you check

Requests arrive as a PRD, a line in Slack, or a problem nobody has written up. The agent sizes the work, then looks for what's already known.

small

A word, a small fix

Nothing to look up. It goes straight to the check.

medium

A change to a place you have

Searches your docs, chats, tickets and numbers, in about ten minutes.

large

A new screen, flow or idea

Also looks at how other products handle the same job.

Every finding has a source and says how sure it is: seen, said or guessed. No PRD? It drafts one for someone to correct.

Some requests come with a PRD, some don't, and they come in every size. So the agent sizes the work first, and you can change its call. The bigger it is, the further it looks: your own docs and chats for a medium change, other products too for a large one. Connect Notion, Slack or Google Drive to Claude Code so it can look for itself. What comes back is a short brief, with a source for every finding, and a PRD draft if there wasn't one. If someone only asked "what do we know?", it stops there.

This is the step most teams skip: a design can follow every rule and still be the wrong thing to build. These three are real runs on the sample PRDs in the repo. The inbox is a go. Guidance tends to pile up in one long document that drifts. Here every question gets one file, in one of two folders: product/ for what to build and why, design-system/ for how it looks and works. When something changes there's one place to edit, and the agent opens a short, named list of files for each task. During a build it reads only the product entries the framing cites. AI Design Harness

8

Building and reviewing are different jobs, so they read different things. Building reads the product entries the framing cites plus the design files, and spreads out into a few real options. For a flow, each option holds every screen and the links between them work. Reviewing first asks whether the design does the job from the framing, then checks one area at a time — components, layout, then copy, meaning the UI text — and only re-reads the files for that area. Every finding points to a rule or principle ID. The rough directions are there so nobody polishes a direction nobody wanted: agreeing on the direction is cheap, building it isn't. Feedback like "option B, but without the dialog" gets a second round next to the first.

AI Design Harness

9

Every finding starts open in work/feedback.md, including observations from support tickets, analytics or interviews. Sort small and often: when a feature merges, sort its rows while people remember them, and let an owner catch the rest once a week. The agent groups them and suggests where each should go, but an owner decides. A row can stay open on purpose with a date to look again. Notes about the harness itself can go to the harness repo as issues, with nothing about your product in them. Adopted ones change product/ or the design system in a pull request, and the decisions file next to it gets a row. Promote when it came up on two or more screens, or the same rule was broken twice, and you can name the one file where it belongs. Screen-only ones stay with that screen. Dropped ones keep their row and the reason.

IN PRACTICE

Running it as a team

Owners are the people in CODEOWNERS: product owners for product/, design owners for design-system/.

First months

Only owners change product/ and the design system. Collect findings first.

Then

Designers open PRs too. Start with UI text rules (writing.md).

As you go

Sort each feature's notes as it merges. An owner catches the rest weekly.

Approvals

With two or more owners, require their approval on GitHub.

NOT FOR

Screens unlike anything your product has today. The agent drafts options; someone with design experience should finish them.

AI Design Harness

13

design-builder starts by looking into the request and framing it, and stops if the framing says stop. Then it walks through what a designer does in their head. The important part is step 5: options have to differ in structure, like list-first versus detail-first, not just in color. For a brand-new kind of screen it stops at the options and a person picks. You'll answer up to five questions along the way. If your product has its own components, the options are built with them on a branch that never merges. These steps are a default, not a script: when a request calls for something else, the agent does that and says why.

APPENDIX

What the check catches

npm run check runs on your machine and on every pull request.

File missing from an INDEX

In product/ or design-system/, or its Answers line doesn't match

Out of sync

Your code changed since the last npm run sync

Off-token CSS

Hard-coded colors or unknown CSS variables (R-01)

Duplicate IDs

Two entries share an ID: U-, J-, PD-, R-, FB- and the rest

Fact or guess?

A user or job not marked fact, hypothesis or open

Old notes

A file not reviewed in 90 days, or a log row open for 30

AI Design Harness

15

If a machine can catch it, the machine should. The check has no dependencies. Lines you tag with a feedback ID are skipped on purpose, so breaking a rule is allowed as long as it is recorded. With sync.json, it also fails when your product's code has changed and the harness hasn't caught up.
